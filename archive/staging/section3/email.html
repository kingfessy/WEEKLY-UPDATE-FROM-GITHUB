LIQUIDITY AND CREDIT

Week ending October 30, 2026
Quick Summary

Funding conditions show mixed signals this week. Short-term rates edged higher while some repo rates slipped. Total bank credit and commercial loans both rose, expanding credit supply. Money supply growth adds to overall liquidity. Deposits at large and small banks climbed, giving banks more funding capacity. Overall, the balance tilts slightly toward looser conditions despite modest rate increases.

SOFR

2026-10-26

SOFR rose to 3.93 percent for 2026-10-26, up 0.06 percentage points from the prior period. The increase pushes short-term borrowing costs higher. This signals a tightening of funding conditions.

EFFR

2026-10-26

IORB

2026-10-26

Total Bank Credit

2026-10-14

Total Bank Credit rose to $20.0 trillion for 2026-10-14, up $45.6 billion from the prior period. The expansion adds to the pool of available credit. This points to looser funding conditions.

Commercial Loans

2026-09-01

Commercial Loans rose to $2.96 trillion for 2026-09-01, up $24.6 billion from the prior period. The growth reflects more lending to businesses. Funding conditions are easing.

DVP Repo Rate

2026-10-28

DVP Repo Rate fell to 3.95 percent for 2026-10-28, down 0.02 percentage points from the prior period. The lower rate reduces the cost of short-term funding. This indicates a loosening of repo market conditions.

Other Updates

Tri-Party Repo Rate rose to 3.94 percent up 0.02 percentage points and GCF Repo Rate rose to 3.97 percent up 0.06 percentage points, indicating marginally tighter repo funding. US M2 Money Supply increased to $23.41 trillion up $113.0 billion, showing abundant liquidity. Treasury General Account grew to $999.7 billion up $47.7 billion while Overnight Reverse Repo fell to $0.2 billion down $0.1 billion, net effect modest tightening in short-term funding. Large Bank Deposits rose to $19.81 trillion up $38.7 billion, Small Bank Deposits to $5.7 trillion up $6.9 billion, and Total Bank Assets to $26.14 trillion up $38.1 billion, suggesting banks have more funding capacity.

What to Watch

Look for the next Fed rate decision to see if policy rates move higher or lower. Monitor weekly changes in the Treasury General Account and repo rates for signs of short-term funding pressure. Track the next release of foreign official Treasury holdings, which arrives with a 1.5-month lag, for shifts in foreign demand.
